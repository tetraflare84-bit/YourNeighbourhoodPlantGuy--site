September 10, 2026 · September Turf Edition Bradford, ON Trevor Auton

Why September is the Ultimate Month for Lawn Winterization

If you only dedicate one month a year to intensive lawn care, make it September. While many homeowners mistakenly believe spring is the prime time for turf maintenance, early autumn offers the perfect combination of warm soil, cool evening temperatures, and reliable moisture. Taking the right steps right now builds deep, drought-proof root systems that will safely sleep through a harsh Ontario winter and wake up thick, green, and weed-free next spring.

Dethatch: Clear the Suffocating Barrier

Over the summer, dead grass stems, roots, and organic debris accumulate between the green blades and the soil surface. Power raking or dethatching in early September strips away this matted sponge, letting your turf breathe and exposing bare soil for incoming seed.

Core Aeration: Relieve Soil Compaction

Summer foot traffic and heavy rains leave Southern Ontario’s clay soils densely compacted. Core aeration pulls 2 to 3-inch soil plugs out of the turf, creating open conduits for roots to stretch deeper into the root zone.

Overseed & Topdress for Dense Turf

September is the golden window for cool-season grasses like Kentucky Bluegrass and Turf-Type Tall Fescues. Soil is warm enough for fast germination without scorching heat drying out tender shoots.

Feed with a Winterizer (Root Strength Over Blade Growth)

A quality fall winterizer fertilizer provides moderate slow-release nitrogen alongside higher levels of Potassium (K), fortifying cellular walls against sub-zero freezing and crown rot over winter.

When to Stop Cutting & The Final Mower Height

On your final cut of the season, gradually drop your deck to roughly 2 to 2.5 inches. Keeping it short prevents tall blades from bending under heavy snowpacks, drastically reducing the risk of destructive snow mold.

Summary & Action Plan

The secret to a show-stopping lawn in May is what you do in September. If you need help with aeration, seed blends, or late-season nutrition, feel free to reach out to Your Neighbourhood Plant Guy!
