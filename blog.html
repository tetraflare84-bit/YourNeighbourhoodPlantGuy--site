Seasonal Advisory
Early Fall Edition Bradford, ON Trevor Auton

Fall Has Come Early!!!

With the consistently cool weather and twice weekly showers in the lovely town we call Bradford, our silent friends the plants are certainly getting ready for an early winter’s slumber. As the daylight shortens and temperatures steadily drop, taking proactive steps right now will dictate how vigorously your trees, shrubs, and perennials wake up next spring.

Prime Timing for Fall Planting & Transplanting

Thanks to the unseasonably cool and moist weather, the standard fall planting window has arrived earlier than expected. Cooler air temps prevent heat stress and transpirational shock on the canopy, allowing new perennials, shrubs, and trees to concentrate 100% of their metabolic energy on root establishment rather than sustaining foliage against scorching heat.

Take Advantage of Well-Hydrated Soil

Our regular rain showers have left the local ground thoroughly hydrated deep down into the root zones. Soil that is consistently damp is much easier to work, dig, and aerate. More importantly, well-hydrated plant tissues and damp root balls insulate significantly better against unexpected early frosts compared to bone-dry autumn soil.

Fortify Root Systems with Bonemeal

High-nitrogen fertilizers should be set aside now so we don't stimulate tender, frost-susceptible green growth. Instead, apply organic Bonemeal. Packed with natural phosphorus and calcium, bonemeal encourages slow, deep root elongation throughout the fall, giving your plants a sturdy anchor and massive nutrient reserves to draw upon before the ground freezes.

Insulate Root Zones with Fresh Mulch

Applying a 2 to 3-inch layer of clean, organic mulch around the drip-line acts as a thermal blanket. Mulch buffers your delicate root systems against rapid temperature swings and harsh "freeze-thaw" cycles that heave roots out of the soil during early winter. Just remember to keep mulch pulled back 2 inches from tree trunks and stems!

Lawn Repair & Season-Closing Fortification

Now is the ultimate window for lawn rehabilitation. Cool nights and warm ground temperatures make ideal conditions for core aeration, overseeding thin patches, and applying a slow-release fall turf fertilizer. Repairing summer stress now guarantees dense, weed-resistant turf when melting snow departs in April.

Diligence with Diseased Plant Material Cleanup

Do not let blighted, spotted, or mildewed leaves sit on your garden beds over the winter. Fungal spores and garden pests happily overwinter in damp leaf debris, ready to re-infect emerging buds next season. Rake, prune, and safely dispose of affected foliage off-site rather than adding it to your backyard compost pile.

Summary & Next Steps

An early fall gives us a head start to safeguard our gardens. By transplanting early, fortifying roots with bonemeal, laying protective mulch, and cleaning up diseased debris, you ensure your yard emerges lush, vibrant, and resilient next spring.
