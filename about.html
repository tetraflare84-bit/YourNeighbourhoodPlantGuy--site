25 Years of Listening to Plants

Twenty-five years ago, I took a job as a labourer for a mid-sized arboriculture provider. Almost immediately, I fell completely in love with the intricate complexities of plants as living, breathing beings with individual, diverse needs.

Although plants stand with a strong, silent stoicism, I quickly learned that they do speak to us — in a much subtler, fascinating language. They communicate through their stature, branch architecture, leaf color, root symptoms, and the minute differences between them and their neighbouring brethren.

"Every plant in its place — thriving in the exact microclimate, soil, and environment it was meant to call home."

Over the past two and a half decades, I have dedicated my life to researching diverse plant varieties, honing the diagnostic skills required to truly read what a stressed plant is trying to tell me. I’ve had the privilege of studying under mentors across a wide range of botanical specialties — absorbing unique perspectives on how to deliver the highest standard of care, whether for a single ornamental specimen or an entire residential landscape.
